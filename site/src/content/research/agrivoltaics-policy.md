This policy gap analysis started during the GIZ Bangladesh GET-PAP internship, and the majority of it was written after the internship. Oishee presented it at the Agrivoltaics World Conference 2026. It reviewed Bangladesh's Renewable Energy Policy 2025, Net Metering Guideline 2025, BERC Grid Code 2023, Electricity Act 2018, and Electricity Rules 2020 to identify regulatory gaps blocking agrivoltaic adoption. The study found that no current instrument defines technical standards, grid-connection pathways, or land-use exemptions for agrivoltaic systems, and recommended targeted regulatory interventions. The paper was co-authored with Md Abdul Gaffar (University of Dhaka, Institute of Energy).

## What the presentation covered

The presentation explained why agrivoltaics matters given Bangladesh's land scarcity and food insecurity, and compared regulation in Germany, Japan and India. It highlighted the lack of a clear definition of agrivoltaics in Bangladesh: SREDA, BERC and the Ministry of Land have no shared definition to apply. It identified four gaps: no technical standards, no grid-integration or licensing pathway, tension between land use and food security, and weak institutional coordination. It recommended a BSTI technical guideline, a licensing and grid-interconnection pathway for farm-scale systems, a land-use exemption conditional on that guideline, and a formal mandate for the national working group.
